# Physical-account ownership checkpoint

This implementation is not PostgreSQL, pooler, crash-recovery or Binance venue qualification. The default CLI deployment continues to reject binding and execution. Do not use the existence of these components as approval to connect an account or submit simulated orders.

## Implemented fail-closed boundary

- Physical identity is the Demo environment, venue, product and verified UID. It excludes strategy and account epoch. A separate physical transaction advisory lock is acquired before the epoch sender lock. Both use dedicated checked-out PostgreSQL connections with open transactions. Rollback releases the transaction lock; a failed rollback invalidates the connection. The physical transaction may commit only to seal a verified CLEAN transition, which simultaneously releases its lock.
- Before account registration or socket opening, an isolated physical owner row is persisted as `DIRTY`, generation 1. A legacy account record matching that UID without a trusted physical mapping blocks acquisition. Missing or malformed legacy identity also blocks rather than being silently inferred. An uncertain commit or lock loss never deletes the row.
- A graceful stop can mark CLEAN only after a subscribed final reconciliation, locally initiated socket close and receiver join, complete inbox drain, durable pause and an unchanged full activity snapshot under both locks. CLEAN alone does not authorize a rebind. Only the original service instance, after receiving a successful commit acknowledgement and closing its transport, retains a one-use, non-serializable in-memory handoff for the same account epoch. Rebind increments generation and credential revision, invalidates qualification and starts in RECOVERY_REQUIRED. A crash, restart, lost acknowledgement, failed close or missing handoff remains blocked even if the database says CLEAN. Do not manually delete or change the row to bypass this barrier.
- The stream and REST backfill carry the bound credential revision and physical generation into durable observations; cursor advancement verifies the same revision and generation. The owner is rechecked around potentially blocking operations. A fact committed just before lock loss remains attached to the old generation and requires recovery; it is not proof of complete coverage.
- On revoked-session or failed graceful teardown, workers and sockets must finish, pending inbox records drain in batches, conflicts remain blocking, and durable pause must succeed before the physical lock is released. UNKNOWN orders and original IDs are not cleared. Without the complete clean proof the row stays DIRTY. A remote socket close is not a locally initiated close proof; unsupported frames are persisted as conflicts.
- Actual sender execution requires a physical owner and a distinct epoch sender lock. Physical and sender lock ownership is rechecked before the durable MAY transition and immediately before the transport sends. A failure after MAY is conservatively UNKNOWN; loss of a database connection cannot retract an HTTP request already sent or forcibly close a socket in another process.
